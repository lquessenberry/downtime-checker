Original calculator from public civil-defense reports. It is not NUKEMAP and it does not use that site’s code. Blast distances use the curve fits in Fletcher, Albright, Perret, Franklin, Bowen, and White, Nuclear Bomb Effects Computer, CEX-62.2, U.S. Atomic Energy Commission, 1963 (equations 2.5, 2.61, 2.78, 2.108, 2.110, 2.111, and 2.116). Fireball size and the crater radius use Glasstone and Dolan, The Effects of Nuclear Weapons, 1977, sections 2.127 and 6.09.

Checked against the published 1 megaton optimum airburst: about 2.8 km at 20 psi, 7.0 km at 5 psi, and 21.7 km at 1 psi. The fits are for roughly 1 kiloton to 20 megatons. Outside that, radii follow the cube-root law. Clear air, flat ground, no terrain, no casualties, and no fallout plume.

15 kilotons is about the yield of the Hiroshima bomb. That line is only a scale marker.
